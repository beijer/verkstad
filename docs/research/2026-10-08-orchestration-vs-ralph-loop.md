# Demote the orchestrator, keep the Landing

Your suspicion is right about the LLM orchestrator and wrong if you stretch it to the whole toolset. In October 2026 the defensible way to run a solo developer's Ticket loop in Claude Code is a **deterministic outer loop**: code claims the next ready Ticket, starts a fresh agent for it, gates it, has an independent agent verify it, lands it and repeats. The parts of verkstad that already work this way are the CLI's frontier, Gate, Verdict, Landing and Park, and they are the parts every practitioner and Anthropic's own guidance converge on. The part that does not earn its cost is the 96-line `skills/orchestrate/SKILL.md` and its prompts. That is about 6,000 words of routing rules a model must hold in its head across a 10-hour Run. It changed in 24 of the repo's 88 commits in six days, and no test covers it. Recommendation: replace the orchestrator session with a `verkstad run` subcommand. It would launch one headless `claude -p` session per Ticket (implementer, then Verifier), read their structured output, route on Landing reason codes in tested TypeScript, and store retry budgets on disk. Keep the Verifier, the review, the Contract and reflect. Move Tier and overlap decisions to triage time, where the owner already looks once. Of the native options, dynamic workflows come closest but fit worse, because a workflow script cannot run git or the CLI itself. Projects and agent teams do not fit a local, Gate-first loop. `/goal` and the ralph-wiggum plugin solve a different, single-session problem. The evidence is thin in one important place: nobody, verkstad included, has measured an orchestrator against a script loop on the same backlog. The first migration step is therefore a one-afternoon experiment, not a rewrite.

## Six days and 88 commits went mostly into the prose state machine

verkstad's history shows where the owner's time went. The repo is **88 commits old, from 2026-10-02 to 2026-10-08**. In that time **`skills/orchestrate/` was touched in 29 commits**, `docs/contract.md` in 28 and `src/land.ts` in 17. **Issues #15–#37 (23 of 37) are follow-ups driven by Runs**, and 17 commits name a Run they came from reflecting on ([git log, verkstad](/home/johan/code/verkstad)). Little of that work is new capability. Most of it fixes friction between three things: the orchestrator's prose, the agents' behaviour, and Claude Code's worktree guard. Some rules flipped within hours. A `gate-flaky` reason was added in ea65905 and removed about 1.5 hours later in c741ed6. "There is no Tier above hard; Park it" became "Resumed on hard" in 8ab70c8. A prose line in the Tier agents was deleted in d404ba9 because agents "broke [it] as often as without it" ([beijer/verkstad#37](https://github.com/beijer/verkstad/issues/37)).

The split in the code explains the churn. About **3,360 lines of TypeScript, backed by about 5,350 lines of process-level tests, own every side effect** on git and GitHub. Every *decision* lives in Markdown that the model must follow. The orchestrator routes on **4 report statuses, 5 Verdict states and 11 Landing reason codes**. It enforces one Resume, one Fix round, a second conflict counting as a Resume and one Verifier retry. It removes stale worktrees by hand before a Resume, judges overlap "lanes" from Ticket text, and picks a Tier per Ticket ([SKILL.md](/home/johan/code/verkstad/skills/orchestrate/SKILL.md)). **No machine-readable record of per-Ticket attempts exists**, so whether the budgets were followed can only be checked by reading transcripts. The meaning of each Landing reason is written three times: in `src/land.ts`, in `docs/contract.md` and in the skill. The review rule is enforced three ways: a prompt line, the SubagentStop hook and Landing's `review-missing` ([verkstad_current_design notes](/home/johan/code/verkstad/src/land.ts)). The repo's own reflect rule, "a rule a skill already states and agents still broke needs a check", has been steadily pushing prose into code. Each new check then adds a reason code that the prose router must handle.

The Run logs show what this costs in operation. cnc-control's largest Run (8dcb572e) took **about 10 hours: 115 agents, 24 Landings including failures, 5 Parks and 236 tool errors**. **23 of its Tickets were dispatched more than once**, one of them five times. The orchestrator's peak context reached **654k tokens**, although the skill tells it to "keep your own context small". Run c71e5498 peaked at 358k ([SKILL.md:7](/home/johan/code/verkstad/skills/orchestrate/SKILL.md); run-log digests). Tool errors are dominated by Claude Code's worktree guard and by verkstad's own 381-line `src/hook.ts`, a shell tokenizer written to predict those guard refusals and explain them. Roughly **170 of the 236 errors** in 8dcb572e were guard or hook refusals. Five rounds of hook and prose changes have not removed them ([src/hook.ts](/home/johan/code/verkstad/src/hook.ts); [beijer/verkstad#29](https://github.com/beijer/verkstad/issues/29)). Tokens are not the main cost. An upper-bound estimate for 8dcb572e's orchestrator is 281 turns times 654k tokens, about 184M cache-read tokens, or under $40 at Opus 5.5's $0.20/MTok cache-read price ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)). The real costs are owner hours spent re-tuning prose, and routing that cannot be tested.

## Everyone converged on fresh context per task plus a deterministic gate

The Ralph loop started as Geoffrey Huntley's July 2025 `while :; do cat PROMPT.md | claude-code; done`. Its load-bearing ideas were never the infinite loop. They are **fresh context per unit of work, file and git state as the only memory, one small item per iteration, and tests as backpressure** ([ghuntley.com/ralph](https://ghuntley.com/ralph/)). Dex Horthy put it bluntly. Anthropic's same-session ralph-wiggum plugin "misses the key point of ralph which is not 'run forever' but in 'carve off small bits of work into independent context windows'" ([HumanLayer](https://www.humanlayer.dev/blog/brief-history-of-ralph)). Anthropic's own long-running harness reached the same shape, and it found that working on "only one feature at a time… turned out to be critical" ([Anthropic, Nov 2025](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents)). Its current prompting docs advise "starting with a brand new context window rather than using compaction", because the latest models rediscover state from the filesystem well ([Anthropic docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)).

Stronger models moved the guidance toward *less* scaffolding, not more. Anthropic's March 2026 harness post says "every component in a harness encodes an assumption about what the model can't do on its own". With Opus 4.6 it dropped sprint decomposition and per-sprint checks, and **kept only the planner and one independent evaluator** ([Anthropic, Mar 2026](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)). The Opus 5 prompting page goes further. It says to remove verification instructions and "legacy harness scaffolding that adds separate verification steps" with "no loss in quality". It also warns that delegation "multiplies cost and time when applied to small tasks" ([Anthropic docs, Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5)). Two other sources still endorse **a separate, fresh-context checker that the harness runs against a specification**: the Fable 5 page ([Anthropic docs, Fable 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-fable-5)) and Claude Code's best-practice docs, which say "the longer Claude works unattended, the more an independent check matters" ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). Read together, the pages reject the model checking itself through a subagent it spawns. They do not reject a harness-run Verifier that never sees the implementer's report, which is exactly what verkstad's `verifier` is. Cognition's April 2026 rule makes the same split from production data: multi-agent works when agents "contribute intelligence to a task while writes stay single-threaded". Its clean-context reviewer finds **about 2 bugs per PR, ~58% of them severe** ([Cognition](https://cognition.com/blog/multi-agents-working)).

On *who decides what runs next*, the sources favour code. Anthropic's founding distinction separates "workflows" (predefined code paths) from "agents" (models directing their own process). It reserves the latter for subtasks that "aren't pre-defined" ([Anthropic, Dec 2024](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). Claude Code's docs say hooks "are deterministic and guarantee the action happens", whereas instructions "are advisory" ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). verkstad's next step is not undefined. It is a lookup over a reason code and an attempt count. The guardrails Ralph practitioners report as essential map onto verkstad's deterministic CLI almost one for one: claim one task, gate before commit, cap iterations, serialise merges when running in parallel ([chrismdp.com](https://www.chrismdp.com/your-agent-orchestrator-is-too-clever/); [FutureAGI](https://futureagi.com/blog/ralph-loop-production-failure-modes/)). Pocock's AFK setup is close to verkstad in function: triage skill, issue queue, Sonnet implementing, Opus reviewing, a merger for parallel branches. The difference is that his control flow is a shell script, not an agent ([aihero.dev](https://www.aihero.dev/tips-for-ai-coding-with-ralph-wiggum); [talk notes](https://github.com/shanraisshan/claude-code-best-practice/blob/main/videos/claude-matt-pocock-24-apr-26.md)).

The Opus 5.5 era does not change this. The launch post cites a customer run "overnight, unattended… over 18 hours" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5-5)), but that is a vendor testimonial. METR called Opus 5.5 "a modest improvement" over Fable 5.1 and published no time horizon ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-09-22-claude-opus-5-5/)). The last published figures, for Opus 4.6, put the **50% horizon near 12 hours but the 80% horizon at about 1 hour 10 minutes** ([METR](https://metr.org/time-horizons/)). Dependable unattended work still comes in Ticket-sized units with an independent check. That supports the per-Ticket unit, whatever runs the loop around it.

## Claude Code's native options each cover only part of the loop

Claude Code now ships five parallel-work mechanisms and several loop mechanisms ([Run agents in parallel](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agents)). Measured against verkstad's needs (local Gate, live Surfaces Walked on the owner's machine, `blocked_by` ordering, serialised Landing, bounded retries ending in a Park), each covers a slice:

| Option | What it does well | Why it is not the loop for verkstad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current LLM orchestrator skill | Judgement on odd cases; native subagents, worktrees, background dispatch | Routing in untested prose; context grows to 300k–650k; every new check adds a prose branch |
| Pure Ralph (one serial session per iteration on the main checkout) | Simplest; no worktree guard, no conflicts | Loses the independent Verifier, ordering and Park unless you add them back, at which point it is `verkstad run` |
| `/goal` (and the older ralph-wiggum plugin) | Native "keep going until X" in one session, works headless | Same-session; the evaluator "doesn't run commands or read files" and only judges the transcript ([/goal docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/goal)), which is weaker backpressure than `verkstad gate` |
| Dynamic workflows | Control flow in JS, schema-typed agent results, resumable, can ship in a plugin; the docs invite converting "a skill that fans work out" ([Workflows docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/workflows)) | The script has no shell or filesystem access, so every `verkstad land` needs an `agent()` wrapper with a model in between; resumable only in the same session; no mid-run input; cannot be tested at verkstad's process seam |
| Projects | Cloud coordinator, a thread per task, PR auto-fix, a merge button | Public beta on Pro/Max only, cloud and GitHub App only; the coordinator is a model and its concurrency limits are "instructions Claude keeps to, not enforced settings" ([Projects docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-projects)); no local Gate or live Surfaces |
| Agent teams | Lead plus a shared task list | Experimental, off by default, no `-p`, and "don't isolate teammates in worktrees" ([Agent teams docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-teams)) |
| `verkstad run` driving `claude -p` | Routing in tested code, no orchestrator context, reuses every CLI guarantee | Loses free-form judgement on odd cases (they Park instead); needs a stub `claude` in tests; relies on headless flags that are still moving |

Dynamic workflows deserve the steelman, because they are Anthropic's own answer to "a script holds the plan instead of Claude's turn-by-turn judgment". If verkstad's side effects were all model actions, a plugin-shipped workflow would be the clear choice. They are not. verkstad's guarantees live in a CLI that a workflow script cannot call. A model would sit between the control flow and `verkstad land`, which brings back the advisory layer that the move to code is meant to remove. Headless Claude Code provides everything the CLI needs to drive sessions itself. That includes `--output-format json`, **`--json-schema` returning a `structured_output`**, `--permission-mode auto` with **`--permission-prompts none` for unattended runs**, and `--resume <id>` to continue a session ([Headless docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/headless)). Since v2.1.246 a turned-out subagent can also be resumed natively instead of being replaced ([Subagents docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)).

## A `verkstad run` command keeps every guarantee and drops the relay

The concrete design: `verkstad run` is a TypeScript loop in `src/run.ts`, under the same test seam as everything else. Each iteration does the following:

1. Read `verkstad frontier --json`, claim a Ticket, create its worktree with `git worktree add`, and run `verkstad start`.
2. Launch `claude -p` in that worktree with the implementing prompt filled in by code, the Tier's model and effort as flags, and a JSON schema for the report: status, surfaces, new surface, uncertain items, and a known-bug flag.
3. If a review record is missing, resume the *same* session with an instruction to review.
4. Compute Surfaces and run a Verifier session with its own schema when any are touched.
5. Call `land`, and route each reason code through a table in code, keeping attempt counts in `<log>/attempts-<n>.json`.

Parking stays `verkstad land --park`. The loop prints one line per transition, and a JSONL event log replaces most of what `run-log`'s 626 lines reconstruct from transcripts today. The owner starts it from a terminal, or from a Claude Code session as a background command watched with Monitor. A `verkstad:run` skill shrinks to a few lines that start it and summarise the event log.

Everything the orchestrator decides moves to one of three places:

| Today (prose, in the orchestrator) | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| Routing on 4 statuses, 5 Verdicts, 11 reason codes; retry budgets | A table in `src/run.ts`, tested against the stub `gh` and a new stub `claude` |
| SendMessage at the turn limit; SubagentStop review hook; Resume with a new agent after `review-missing` | `claude -p --resume <session>` with the specific instruction |
| Manual `git worktree remove -f -f` before a Resume | Code, before relaunching |
| Tier picked per Ticket at dispatch | A Tier (or effort) label set at triage, default standard; code escalates on `partial` |
| Lanes judged from Ticket text | A `blocked_by` relation added at triage when two Tickets share feature code, which is native and fits ADR 0006 |
| "A `done` whose Uncertain names a known bug is not done" | A schema field that code routes on |
| Plan-and-confirm table | `verkstad run --plan` prints it; the owner confirms by running |

Keep the Verifier and its blindness to the implementer's report. Of all the roles, it has the strongest support in the sources. Keep `verkstad:review` and `verkstad:tdd`. Keep the Contract and the deterministic CLI unchanged, and fix #36 in Landing as planned. Keep reflect, now pointed mostly at the routing table and the prompts. Keep the triage, tickets and spec skills. Human planning up front is where Horthy, Pocock and Anthropic's planner agent all put judgement ([LinearB](https://linearb.io/blog/dex-horthy-humanlayer-rpi-methodology-ralph-loop)). Delete the routing body of `skills/orchestrate/SKILL.md`. Keep `implement-prompt.md`, `verify-prompt.md` and `conflict-prompt.md` as templates the CLI fills. Then delete `src/subagent-stop.ts` and its hook. Delete `src/hook.ts` only if the experiment below shows the guard no longer fires.

The steelman for keeping the orchestrator is judgement. It reads triage comments, notices an odd report and picks a sensible lane, and Opus 5.5 is advertised as delegating "far more effectively". The answer is that most of those judgements are already scripted as rules in the prose, so the model mostly relays. The genuinely open ones, such as a blocker needing a decision or an ambiguous report, already end in a Park for the owner. Reading the latest Agent Brief is something the implementer already does. The bitter-lesson critics would also cut the Tier router itself, since it encodes a human taxonomy ([Traversal](https://www.traversal.com/blog/ai-agent-architecture-mistakes)). Anthropic now names effort, not model choice, as "your primary control for token cost" ([Anthropic docs, Opus 5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5)). Collapsing light/standard/hard into one implementer with a triage-set effort level is consistent with that guidance. Its cost effect is unmeasured.

A side benefit could be large but is unverified. Claude Code applies its worktree guard to subagents dispatched with `isolation: worktree`, and since v2.1.210 to a main conversation "when it runs isolated" ([Subagents docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sub-agents)). A plain `claude -p` whose working directory is a worktree that verkstad created may not count as isolated. If it does not, most of the ~170 refusals per large Run, the 381-line hook and the notes-path confusion all go away together. If it does count, nothing is lost relative to today.

## Migration order: one experiment, then serial, then parallel

| Step | Do | Exit criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Freeze | Stop reflect-driven edits to `skills/orchestrate/SKILL.md`; land #36 | No new prose routing rules |
| 1. Experiment (an afternoon) | By hand, run one cnc-control Ticket through a script-made worktree and `claude -p --json-schema … --permission-mode auto --permission-prompts none`, with plugin skills loaded and the log directory added as a working directory | Plugin skills (tdd, review) load; structured output parses; count guard refusals against today's ~1–2 per agent |
| 2. Serial `verkstad run` | One Ticket at a time: frontier, implement, resume-for-review, Verify, land, route, Park; budgets on disk; stub `claude` in `test/stub/`; event log | Tested routing table; one real backlog drained without an orchestrator session |
| 3. Measure | Run it on a cnc-control Spec and record Tickets landed per hour, Parks, tool errors and cost against 8dcb572e and c71e5498 | A number, which no one has today |
| 4. Delete | Orchestrate routing prose, SubagentStop hook, the duplicated reason semantics in the skill; `hook.ts` if step 1 showed no guard | Skill reduced to a launcher |
| 5. Parallel N > 1 | A worker pool in code, a default cap of 2–4, overlap via triage-set `blocked_by`, conflicts to the conflict prompt | Parallel throughput beats serial by enough to pay for conflicts and `verdict-void` re-Walks |
| 6. Optional | Lift review out of the implementer into its own fresh session; try one implementer with effort labels instead of three Tier agents | A/B on Parks and cost |

Serial comes before parallel on purpose. Running one Ticket at a time removes conflicts, lanes, `push-failed` retries, rebase-voided Verdicts and the conflict agent all at once. Every source agrees that parallel loops are where simple loops break down ([chrismdp.com](https://www.chrismdp.com/your-agent-orchestrator-is-too-clever/); [paddo.dev](https://paddo.dev/blog/gastown-two-kinds-of-multi-agent/)). Ray's own history shows five agents at once overloading the machine and its e2e Gates. verkstad needs a measured reason before taking that cost back on. Horthy's cheaper fallback for conflicts is worth a trial in step 5: discard the branch and re-run the Ticket on fresh code ([HumanLayer](https://www.humanlayer.dev/blog/brief-history-of-ralph)).

## Where the evidence is thin

Five gaps limit how far the recommendation can be trusted.

- **No comparison on the same backlog.** No source compares an LLM orchestrator with a script loop of fresh sessions on the same backlog, so the case against the orchestrator rests on verkstad's own churn and logs plus converging expert guidance, not on a controlled result. The one large instrumented loop, Carlini's C compiler, used **16 parallel fresh-context loops and no orchestrator: ~2,000 sessions, ~$20k, 99% of the GCC torture tests**. It had a test oracle far stronger than any app's acceptance criteria ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-c-compiler)).
- **Throughput and cost per Ticket.** verkstad has never measured throughput or cost per Ticket, serial or parallel.
- **The Verifier's value.** Its value is supported by principle more than by verkstad's data. cnc-control holds **45 live-verified Verdicts against 1 failed**, and how many real bugs the Verifier caught that the Gate would have missed was never recorded.
- **Tier savings.** Tiering's cost savings are unmeasured, and no controlled study compares a cheap model on easy Tickets with one model at lower effort.
- **Unverified platform behaviour.** Whether the worktree guard applies to a `claude -p` session in an externally created worktree is untested. So is what the future `--bare` default for `-p` will strip ([Headless docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/headless)). Opus 5.5's long-run claims are vendor testimonials.
- **Conflicting Anthropic guidance.** Anthropic's own pages disagree on verification subagents (Opus 5 versus Fable 5). The reading that separates harness-run verification from self-spawned verification is an interpretation, not a stated rule.

## Conclusion

Asking "orchestrator or Ralph loop?" frames the choice wrongly. In Anthropic's terms, verkstad already contains a good *workflow*: the CLI. A model sits on top of it acting as a slow, untestable interpreter for that workflow's routing table. The owner's time went into keeping that interpreter in line with the code, which is why each lesson from reflect turned into another prose rule and another reason code. Moving the router into `src/run.ts` changes what reflect produces. A lesson that used to become a sentence the model might ignore becomes a tested branch. That is the end point the repo's own rule ("a lesson seen twice becomes a script or a check") was already heading toward.

Model judgement still belongs in three places: implementing a Ticket, verifying it blind against its criteria, and planning with the owner before the Run starts. Stronger models make each of those cheaper. They do not make a model-run dispatcher worth its context, and Anthropic's 2026 guidance says to strip components like it as models improve. Spend the next afternoon on the step 1 experiment. If plugin skills load and structured output holds up in `claude -p`, the orchestrator skill is a candidate for deletion within a week.
